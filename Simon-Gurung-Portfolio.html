A1 Brand Identity

Kholaghar Coffee — Brand Identity

A full identity system for a small-batch coffee roastery sourcing beans from the hills of Nepal — mark, palette, type and packaging built to work from a cup sleeve to an export crate.

  • RoleBrand & Identity Designer

Challenge

Kholaghar sources beans from small hill farms and roasts in small batches, but the original identity looked like a generic café sticker. It needed to read as premium on an export crate and still feel handmade on a single cup sleeve.

Approach

The mark combines a bean silhouette with a single river line, a direct nod to "Kholaghar" (riverside house). A warm, food-safe palette carries across matte packaging, while a restrained two-typeface system keeps every touchpoint legible at small sizes.

Outcome

The system now covers bags, cartons, cups, stationery and a wholesale line sheet from one shared set of rules, cutting new-SKU design time from days to hours for the in-house team.

B2 UX / UI Design

Yatra — Intercity Bus Booking App

An end-to-end UX/UI project for booking intercity bus tickets in Nepal — route search, seat selection and e-ticketing designed to feel as simple as booking a flight.

  • RoleProduct Designer, UX/UI
  • DeliverablesUser flows, wireframes, hi-fi UI kit

Challenge

Booking an intercity bus in Nepal usually means calling a counter or trusting a reseller with no seat map. Riders needed to see real seat availability and hold a ticket on their phone with no back-and-forth.

Approach

Wireframes cut the journey to three screens: search, seat map, ticket. A visual seat grid replaces a dropdown so availability is understood at a glance, and the confirmation screen doubles as an offline-readable ticket stub.

Outcome

Usability sessions with 8 riders showed a first booking completed in under 90 seconds, down from several minutes of back-and-forth on the counter-call flow it replaced.

C3 Website Redesign

Mahakavi Devkota Campus — Website Redesign

A ground-up redesign of Simon's own alma mater's website — turning a cluttered, notice-board-style homepage into a clear, mobile-friendly front door for students and staff.

  • RoleUI Designer / Front-end
  • DeliverablesIA, new UI, responsive build

Challenge

The old site buried admissions info, notices and faculty contacts behind a wall of top navigation links and dense text blocks, and broke completely on mobile, where most students actually visited from.

Approach

A content audit cut the navigation from eleven items to four. Notices moved to a dedicated feed instead of the homepage, and a single clear hero replaced the old text banner, with a mobile-first grid underneath everything.

Outcome

The redesigned homepage loads in a fraction of the old page weight, and the new notice feed gives staff a way to publish updates without touching page layout.

D4 Motion Graphics

Kholaghar — Logo Reveal & Promo Motion

A looping logo-reveal animation and a six-frame storyboard for a short social promo, extending the Kholaghar brand system (project A1) into motion for reels and in-store screens.

  • RoleMotion Designer
  • DeliverablesLogo animation, storyboard, social cutdown

Challenge

Kholaghar needed a short, sound-optional sting that worked as a reel intro, an in-store loop, and a seasonal promo teaser, without commissioning a separate animation for each.

Approach

The line-drawn mark from the brand system became the animation's structural device: it draws itself in, fills with the brand palette, then hands off to a product shot. The same six-beat storyboard was cut to 3s, 6s and 15s versions.

Outcome

One storyboard produced three timed edits reused across Instagram, in-store screens and a Dashain promo push, keeping every touchpoint visually identical to the brand system.

Paisa — Everyday Budgeting App

A design system and dashboard for a budgeting app built for young Nepalis managing money across cash, e-wallets and bank accounts — one clear view of where money goes each month.

  • RoleProduct Designer
  • DeliverablesDesign system, dashboard UI, data viz

Challenge

Most budgeting apps assume a single bank account. Paisa's users pay with cash, mobile wallets and cards in the same week, and needed one honest picture of spend without manually categorising every entry.

Approach

A compact design system (spacing scale, chart components, category colours) powers a dashboard that leads with the number that matters, this week's spend against budget, with category and trend views one tap away, not the default screen.

Outcome

The component library cut new-screen design time significantly for the two-person product team, and the budget-progress pattern shown here shipped as the app's most-used screen in early testing.
